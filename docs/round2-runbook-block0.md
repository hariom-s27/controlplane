## §02 — Block 0, closed: the two boundary papers and the theorem

*This was the highest-risk unknown in the project and the roadmap's own gate condition: if Block 0 goes badly, the positioning changes. Here is what the papers actually say, and what survives.*

### arXiv:2608.01050 — Wix, deployed production

**"Don't Offer What Can't Be Done: Deterministic Executability Gating for LLM Skill Selection at Scale."** Ashkenazi, Kloz, Ulianchenko.

| Field | What the paper does |
|---|---|
| Mechanism | Three stages in Helpmate, Wix's customer-care assistant: (1) semantic matching finds relevant skills *without* checking account state; (2) an executability gate removes skills whose hard-stop conditions are unmet against fresh account state; (3) the LLM chooses from what survives. |
| Predicate | **Executability / feasibility.** "Can this skill run for this account right now?" Entitlements, ownership, configuration. |
| Where in the loop | **Before the model decides.** It narrows the menu. |
| Numbers | 756.6K production messages. Semantic matching retains 23.1% of messages; the gate then removes **59.4% of skill–message pairs**, saving 228.8M skill-description tokens; combined 90.5% context reduction. Counterfactual: without the gate the model picked a production-blocked skill in **7.8%** of replayed conversations. |
| What it declines to claim | Correctness. Customer-outcome effects. The framing is as much a context/token-efficiency result as a safety one. |

### arXiv:2607.07405 — the closer of the two

**"Reason Less, Verify More: Deterministic Gates Recover a Silent Policy-Violation Failure Mode in Tool-Using LLM Agents."** Reddy, Challaram, Basu.

| Field | What the paper does |
|---|---|
| Mechanism | Deterministic, **read-only pre-execution gates** that validate a proposed action against current state before the tool executes. |
| Predicate | **Policy conformance / domain-rule validity.** "Silent wrong-state" writes — a cancelled booking, an unverified claim — that look like success. |
| Where in the loop | **After the model proposes, before the tool runs.** The same point ControlPlane sits. |
| Numbers | 78% of observed agent failures are silent wrong-state. Full-benchmark success 29.6% → 42.0% on gpt-4o-mini (**+12.4pp, p=0.0012**), concentrated on the 26 of 50 tasks where gates fired. gpt-5.2: 61.2% → 71.6% (**+10.4pp**) — the failure persists at the frontier. |
| The limit | A **simulated τ²-bench airline database**, not a real system of record. No evidence artifact. No cross-system reconciliation. No treatment of the state source as itself fallible. |

> **The paragraph to write, and to say before anyone else raises it**
>
> Two independent 2026 results now confirm the mechanism. Reddy et al. show that deterministic read-only gates on the current state recover the dominant silent-failure mode in tool-using agents — **+12.4 points, and the gap does not close at the frontier**. Wix show the same class of check running in production against live business state across 756,641 messages. **We cite both, generously, because they are the strongest available evidence that this works.**
>
> Four things separate ControlPlane from both. **One:** the predicate. Reddy et al. ask whether the state change is *permitted* given current state; Wix asks whether a skill *can run*. Neither asks whether the agent's factual assertion is *contradicted by an independent authority*. **Two:** the direction of the check. Wix narrows the menu before the model chooses; we check the order after it is placed — the only point at which the agent's own claim exists to be falsified. **Three:** neither emits an evidence artifact. A gate that blocks and moves on cannot be defended to a regulator; the Decision Receipt can. **Four:** neither models its own source as fallible. Reddy et al. run against a simulated database. We ship SOURCE-UNRELIABLE as a fourth verdict class because we measured that the record is sometimes wrong.

**Verdict on the gate condition: the positioning survives, narrowed and better-evidenced.** Drop any phrasing implying nobody does pre-execution state checks — two teams do, one in production. Keep the predicate-class claim, and lead with the receipt and the source-reliability axis, which are genuinely unclaimed.

### The Deming citation — real, unread, and probably mis-framed

| Item | Status |
|---|---|
| Papadakis, "The Deming Inspection Criterion for Choosing Zero or 100 Percent Inspection," *Journal of Quality Technology* 17(3), 1985 | **Exists** Title and venue confirmed. Full text 403 at Taylor & Francis. |
| Vardeman, "Mood's Theorem, Deming's kp Rule, and the Death of Acceptance Sampling," *Quality Engineering* 8(1), 1995 | **Exists** Title confirmed and *stronger than the log records*. Full text 403. |
| The sentence built on them | **At risk** See below. |

The standard formulation of the kp rule is: with inspection cost k1 per item and downstream failure cost k2 per defective, and a process **in statistical control** at a stable average defect rate p, minimum total cost is achieved at **0% inspection when p < k1/k2 and 100% when p > k1/k2** — never a fraction. The all-or-none result is proved for the *stable, independent-defect* case.

The current pitch sentence says the opposite — that the theorem applies *because* AI errors are correlated. **That reads as an inversion of the assumption set, and a quality-engineering-literate judge will catch it.** Fortunately the underlying argument does not need the theorem to do that work:

> **Safe reformulation, which survives either reading:** Acceptance sampling rests on operating-characteristic curves, and OC curves assume binomial or hypergeometric draws at a constant proportion nonconforming — i.i.d. defects from a process in statistical control. A superseded policy document is a textbook *assignable cause*: it produces the same error on every retrieval that touches it, which violates the i.i.d. assumption directly rather than merely straining it. And Deming's own inspection criterion says that even in the well-behaved stable case, the cost-optimal policy is zero or everything — never the 1–5% that contact-centre QA actually runs. **Sampling theory says sampling is the wrong tool here, and it says so twice, for two different reasons.**

**Action:** get both papers through a university library or interlibrary loan and read the theorem statement, or move to the reformulation above and cite Papadakis only for the all-or-none criterion. Do not put the current wording in front of a judge. Given four fabricated figures already caught in this project's own work, this is the one remaining citation that could cost more than it earns.

  face needs a single line, use a **verbatim contiguous sentence** from the
  paragraph — e.g. *"Sampling theory says sampling is the wrong tool here, and it

  speaker notes.

  to a theorem.
