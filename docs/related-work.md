# Related work

## Pre-execution enforcement

**AEGIS.** Yuan, Su, and Zhao's *AEGIS: No Tool Call Left Unchecked — A Pre-Execution Firewall and Audit Layer for AI Agents* ([arXiv:2603.12621v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12621), 2026-03-13) interposes a firewall on the tool-execution path, performs composable policy validation, escalates high-risk calls to human approval, and produces an Ed25519-signed, SHA-256 hash-chained audit trail. It overlaps ControlPlane on interception, composable policy checks, and intervention. AEGIS's audit integrity is stronger than ControlPlane's: ControlPlane's decision receipt is HMAC-authenticated with a shared secret and is not hash-chained across entries (`docs/threat-model.md` T24). ControlPlane's receipt design is not presented here as a differentiator from AEGIS. The colliding identifier `2603.16938` names an unrelated paper and is not used for these claims.

**Open Agent Passport (OAP).** Uchibeke's *Before the Tool Call: Deterministic Pre-Action Authorization for Autonomous AI Agents* ([arXiv:2603.20953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20953), 2026-03-21) describes synchronous interception before execution, declarative pre-action authorization, and a cryptographically signed audit record (verified at abstract level; the linked specification repository was not separately inspected). OAP's decision concerns whether a principal is authorized to perform a call; ControlPlane's checks concern whether specific proposed call content agrees with a resolved record. Both sit at the same pre-execution boundary, so the boundary itself is not a distinction between them.

**Reason Less, Verify More.** Reddy, Challaram, and Basu's *Reason Less, Verify More: Deterministic Gates Recover a Silent Policy-Violation Failure Mode in Tool-Using LLM Agents* ([arXiv:2607.07405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07405), 2026-07-08) uses deterministic, read-only pre-execution gates that inspect the proposed call together with current simulated database state, evaluated on tau2-bench-style retail tasks and BFCL negative controls. This is the nearest prior system on mechanism and benchmark family. Its reported effect sizes are not compared numerically to anything in this repository — different populations, protocols, and denominators make any such comparison **NOT DIRECTLY COMPARABLE**.

**AgentCore Policy and Policies on Paths.** The official [Amazon Bedrock AgentCore Policy developer guide](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/policy-core-concepts.html) documents deterministic enforcement performed outside agent code, evaluating identity, tool schema, session events, and guardrail signals before tool access, with decisions logged to CloudWatch. The fetched pages do not document a business-record re-query mechanism or a named `AUDIT`/`ENFORCE` mode pair; that is an absence in the pages reviewed, not evidence that no such mechanism exists. Kaptein, Khan, and Podstavnychy's *Runtime Governance for AI Agents: Policies on Paths* ([arXiv:2603.16586v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16586), 2026-03-17) permits prospective policy decisions to depend on agent identity, partial path, proposed action, and shared governance state Σ. Consulting state beyond the immediate call at decision time is therefore already established prior art; Σ is a governance ledger, distinct from the transactional business record that a specific claim concerns.

Pre-execution enforcement of agent tool calls is consequently an active, multi-system area — AEGIS, OAP, AgentCore Policy, and Reason Less, Verify More all occupy it — and is not a ControlPlane-specific contribution.

## Trace-grounded verification

**AgentLTL.** Elkoussy and Perez's *AgentLTL: A Trace-Verification Framework for Measuring, Enforcing, and Training Procedural Compliance in Tool-Using LLM Agents* ([arXiv:2607.02599v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02599), 2026-07-01) supports both offline scoring and online prefix gating (`HARD_STOP`/`SOFT_BLOCK`/`BLOCK_AND_WARN`/`TOLERATE`) against first-order temporal-logic procedural constraints. Its grounding condition, κ_ground, holds when the entities referenced by an action were observed in the agent's own prior tool outputs; the paper states this grounding predicate uses no external data source (source: §8). Pre-execution interception is therefore not unique to ControlPlane, and neither is having a grounding condition — AgentLTL already has one. The distinction under test is narrower: which evidence a check consults. AgentLTL's κ_ground treats the trace itself as the witness; ControlPlane's registry resolvers instead query a configured record store at the moment of adjudication.

**C-Trace.** Kahani, Barati, and Addae's *Runtime Compliance Verification for AI Agents* ([arXiv:2606.19242v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19242), 2026-06-17) is an interceptor around the tool-calling loop that can forward, redact, or block, evaluating four first-order GDPR compliance predicates incrementally over a typed event trace, with adversarial evaluation across four domains including retail and airline and four attack families (source: Table 4). Runtime interception and blocking are present in C-Trace, and ControlPlane's `ALLOW`/`MODIFY`/`BLOCK` vocabulary is not a new framing relative to C-Trace's forward/redact/block. The predicate subject differs: C-Trace evaluates trace-level data-governance obligations; ControlPlane evaluates whether proposed call content agrees with a transactional record.

**LEDGER.** Kim, Miao, and Liu's *LEDGER: Layered Evidence and Decision Graphs for Execution Review* ([arXiv:2608.18398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18398), 2026-08-19) is a post-hoc tracing and review system that builds layered trace graphs over observed agent sessions, grouping trace records into evidence nodes and workflow nodes and adding typed semantic edges that connect claims to supporting actions, artifacts, and checks, for evidence-centered audit review after the fact (source: abstract). The "claim-to-evidence" framing is therefore already published research vocabulary; ControlPlane's structural separation of claimed fields from predicate facts (`ProposedAction.facts_for_predicate()`) is not presented as introducing that framing. The mechanisms differ in kind and timing: LEDGER reconstructs claim-evidence links for a reviewer after a session ends, while ControlPlane's separation operates online, before the action executes, to decide it.

## Maintained structured state

**LedgerAgent.** Uddin, Saeidi, Blanco, and Baral's *LedgerAgent: Structured State for Policy-Adherent Tool-Calling Agents* ([arXiv:2606.20529](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20529)) maintains a typed ledger L: P → V mapping a fixed, developer-defined set of canonical schema paths to tool-returned values; it is explicitly not long-term memory, not an LLM-generated summary, and does not claim to reconstruct unobserved world state (source: §3.1). The ledger updates only from successful read-tool returns — the return is associated with its originating call, parsed, and stored at its canonical path — while failed calls and write-tool returns do not update it; after an environment-changing write, the agent must issue a further read to observe the new state, an "observe-not-assume" rule (source: §3.1). Before each model call the full ledger is rendered deterministically into the prompt alongside the existing conversation history, policy text, and tool schemas (source: §3.2). Immediately before an environment-changing call, a policy gate evaluates deterministic, developer-defined predicates over ledger fields (examples given include ownership, entity-state preconditions, argument grounding, refund/payment consistency, and loop prevention) and returns `ALLOW`, `REVISE` (the rejected call is removed and feedback returned), or `BLOCK`, checking multiple calls individually (source: §3.3). The paper states the gate is only a verifier — it does not choose tools, repair arguments, fetch missing records, or replan — and that predicates use only records already present in the ledger (source: §3.3, §8). Evaluated across airline (50 tasks), retail (114), telecom (114), and telehealth (20) domains, with six agent backbones, temperature 0.0, a fixed user simulator, four trials per task, and pass^1/pass^4 reporting (source: §4), the paper reports improved pass^k relative to its standard prompt-based baseline, including on environment-changing tasks (source: §5). Its own stated limitations are that the ledger contains only observed state, cannot certify facts the agent has not retrieved, is updated only by a subsequent read after a write, depends on developer-maintained schema and predicate definitions, is not automatic policy induction, is not a complete proof of policy compliance, and that the evaluation does not cover live users, adversarial behavior, changing policies, or production traffic (source: §8).

This is the closest problem-level overlap identified in this review: both systems check state-dependent constraints immediately before an environment-changing call, both are motivated by acting on stale or incorrect context, and both are evaluated in customer-service-adjacent domains. Nothing in the paper, including its own limitations section, describes LedgerAgent as lacking governance, enforcement, auditability, or constraint expressiveness, and no such absence is claimed here. The distinction stated is narrower and specific to evidence provenance and timing: LedgerAgent's ledger is populated only by what the agent has already observed through its own prior tool calls, and its gate checks the proposed action against that self-observed record without querying anything further (source: §3.1, §3.3, §8). ControlPlane's registry resolvers instead issue a query against a configured record source at the moment of adjudication, independent of whether the agent called a read tool first. Where that configured source is the same store the agent's own tools would read — the orders and entitlements paths — this is fresh re-derivation from the same underlying source, not source independence; only the policy-clause path queries a source distinct from the agent's own retrieval index (see Evidence and limitations). This describes a difference in when, and by whom, the record is queried — not a claim that LedgerAgent's mechanism is deficient.

## Auditable and accountable agents

**Auditable Agents.** Nian, Yuan, Zhang, Li, Li, Hu, Wei, Xiao, Xiao, and Zhao's *Auditable Agents* ([arXiv:2604.05485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05485)) defines auditability as the system property that makes accountability possible, and operationalizes it as five dimensions — action recoverability, lifecycle coverage, policy checkability, responsibility attribution, and evidence integrity — together with three mechanism classes whose constraints explain why no single approach covers all five: detect, enforce, recover (source: abstract). Scored honestly against those five dimensions, without inflating partial coverage:

| Dimension | What ControlPlane provides | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Action recoverability | A decision receipt is written per adjudicated action | Partial — receipts are HMAC-authenticated per entry but not hash-chained across entries, and the idempotency ledger is in-process and does not survive a restart (`docs/threat-model.md` T22, T24) |
| Lifecycle coverage | Tool calls routed through `dispatch_tool()` are governed pre-execution | Partial — this is a code-structure discipline enforced by tests, not a process or privilege boundary; code that does not call the dispatch path is not covered (`docs/threat-model.md`, Execution interception row) |
| Policy checkability | Policies are declarative, manifest-driven predicate graphs, checked by a use-case-agnostic engine | Directly established — enforced by `tests/test_engine_is_use_case_agnostic.py` |
| Responsibility attribution | Receipts record which predicate and which evidence source produced a verdict | Not established beyond decision-level attribution — no principal- or session-level authorization comparable to OAP |
| Evidence integrity | Each receipt is authenticated with a shared-secret HMAC | Partial and weaker than AEGIS's Ed25519-signed, SHA-256 hash-chained design; not claimed as a contribution |

This is a self-assessment against an externally defined rubric, not an external audit, and it does not establish superiority over any named system.

**Provenance survey.** Wang, Zhang, Wu et al.'s *From Agent Traces to Trust* ([arXiv:2606.04990v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04990), 2026-06-28) surveys evidence tracing and execution provenance across roughly fifty systems along six taxonomy dimensions (source: §2.1). Verification of an agent's claim against an authoritative external system of record is not among the categories that taxonomy enumerates; the survey's evidence-attribution dimension instead concerns alignment with retrieved documents and cited sources (source: §3.2). This is stated as a documented absence in one named survey's taxonomy, not as proof that no such mechanism exists elsewhere or that ControlPlane is first to it — LedgerAgent and Reason Less, Verify More, discussed above, both already consult state beyond the agent's immediate request.

## Where ControlPlane sits

The repository implements a specific pre-execution composition, not a new mechanism class:

- `ProposedAction.facts_for_predicate()` structurally excludes prose-derived `claimed_*` fields, retaining only structural tool-call arguments (`tests/test_predicates.py::test_no_claimed_field_reaches_the_engine`).
- Registry resolvers obtain evidence from configured SQLite record sources at adjudication time; the policy resolver selects the currently effective clause and refuses ambiguous current state.
- Missing, malformed, or unavailable evidence is represented as uncertainty rather than silently passing, following the manifest's compensability-specific fail posture.
- The `dispatch_tool()` interception boundary maps decisions to execution, applies process-local idempotency, and emits an HMAC-authenticated decision receipt.

Each element of that composition is individually established prior art:

| Element | Covered by |
|---|---|
| Pre-execution interception | AEGIS, OAP, AgentCore Policy, AgentLTL |
| Deterministic, non-LLM adjudication | Reason Less, Verify More; C-Trace; AgentLTL; LedgerAgent's policy gate |
| Declarative, data-driven policy | AgentCore Policy (Cedar/Dogwood); AgentLTL (FO-LTL); C-Trace (first-order GDPR predicates); LedgerAgent (developer-defined ledger predicates) — several more expressive than ControlPlane's manifest expression list |
| Blocking / intervention vocabulary | C-Trace's forward/redact/block; LedgerAgent's ALLOW/REVISE/BLOCK |
| Signed audit receipt | AEGIS (hash-chained), OAP — both stronger than ControlPlane's |
| Claim-to-evidence framing | LEDGER |
| Pre-execution state-dependent constraints checked against maintained state | LedgerAgent |
| Consulting state beyond the immediate request | Policies on Paths, Reason Less, Verify More |

What is not established as already covered by any reviewed source, restricted to what the repository's regression tests actually demonstrate: adjudicating a proposed action against a record queried from a configured store at the moment of decision — independent of whether the agent itself already read that record — including selecting the currently effective version of a policy clause independently of the agent's own asserted version. LedgerAgent's gate checks proposed actions only against records the agent has itself already observed and absorbed into its ledger (§3.1, §3.3); it does not query further. Even so, this ControlPlane property is qualified, not absolute: for the orders and entitlements paths the configured store is the same store the agent's own tools would read, so the practical difference from LedgerAgent there is timing and mechanism (a resolver query versus an absorbed prior read), not an independent source — see Evidence and limitations. This is an implementation and design position, not a demonstration of a new mechanism class.

## Evidence and limitations

This document positions an implementation, not an empirical result. Statements about ControlPlane refer to the verified local release candidate represented by the audited HEAD; the public baseline remains `6ec4261d374904f55bf5dff1a9855854f1b94819`. The candidate carries the C/I/D reconciliation from ancestor `986b65e522091cc2d5d2f4ccf571688a095c3529`, the recipient-authorization correction from `dce2e4cc4c0cc1eaae2bc58fc02f5ea7971b65c6`, and the threat-model synchronization from `aac3bea9a7a11e8d9da5173d7e7d647f5f607989`, the Step 6B endpoint and immediate engineering parent — none of these three is itself the current candidate identity, which moves with HEAD. The off-release P04, P05/A1–A5, P08, and P09 results are not evidence for the current candidate, because they have not been rerun against it. P06 and tau2-bench results are not used in this document.

The LedgerAgent section above draws on the full paper (§3.1–§3.4, §4, §5, §8), read directly for this document, not solely on the abstract-level treatment in the internal R02 prior-art matrix.

A decision-time re-query is not automatically independent of the agent's own information source. In the orders and entitlements paths, the agent's tools and ControlPlane's resolver ultimately read the same underlying SQLite store — this is fresh re-derivation from the same source, not source independence, and it is the same qualification that applies to the LedgerAgent comparison above. Only the policy-clause path queries a source that is genuinely distinct from the agent's own retrieval index. Both included use cases (servicing, knowledge-assistant) are synthetic.

Regression tests establish implemented behavior; they do not establish benchmark efficacy, external validity, broad generalization, comparative performance, adversarial robustness, or deployment readiness. The Python interception boundary can be bypassed by code that does not call it; idempotency does not survive a process restart or span processes; receipts are HMAC-authenticated but not hash-chained or otherwise tamper-evident across entries; and the policy representation carries no temporal or formal-verification guarantee.

ControlPlane focuses on pre-execution checks of selected proposed tool-call content against configured records re-queried at the moment of adjudication. The closest prior systems address adjacent but distinct problems: AEGIS and OAP firewall and authorize the tool-execution path with signed audit trails stronger than ControlPlane's; C-Trace and AgentCore Policy intercept and block on typed trace or policy state; AgentLTL grounds procedural constraints in the agent's own trace output; LedgerAgent checks state-dependent constraints against a ledger populated only by the agent's own prior observations; LEDGER reconstructs claim-to-evidence links after a session for review. ControlPlane's implementation separates prose-derived assertions from predicate facts and issues a query against a configured record store at adjudication time, independent of whether the agent read that record first — for the policy-clause path this is a genuinely different source than the agent's own retrieval index, while for order and entitlement data it is a fresh, correctly scoped read of the same store the agent already read. The repository demonstrates this composition on two synthetic use cases within the stated scope. It does not establish novelty, broad generalization, external validation, superiority over any named system, or production readiness.
